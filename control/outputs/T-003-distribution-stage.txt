# Distribution Analysis Stage

## Purpose

Determine the cheapest, fastest, and most verifiable path to reach qualified buyers for an opportunity that has already passed **Market Evidence review**.

This stage does **not** assume that a competitor’s presence proves customer acquisition. It separates:

- **Verified channel evidence:** observable evidence that a competitor or comparable provider uses a channel and appears to generate buyer activity.
- **Directional evidence:** suggestive but incomplete evidence.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible channel with no meaningful verification yet.

The stage must prioritize real-world validation over desk research and must operate within the current approximate testing budget of **$200 total** unless a human approves additional spend.

---

# REQUIRED INPUTS

Distribution Analysis may run only when the opportunity has a passed Market Evidence review.

## 1. Opportunity definition

Required:

- Product or service description
- Target customer segment
- Buyer role and decision-maker
- Core problem being solved
- Primary use case
- Geography or service area
- Expected price or pricing range
- Delivery constraints
- Existing product assets
- Current founder/operator availability

## 2. Market Evidence review output

Required:

- Market Evidence status: `PASSED`
- Evidence supporting demand
- Customer pain points
- Customer language and terminology
- Known alternatives and competitors
- Evidence of willingness to pay, if available
- Unresolved assumptions

No Distribution Analysis should begin if Market Evidence status is:

- `FAILED`
- `INCOMPLETE`
- `UNVERIFIED`

## 3. Distribution constraints

Required:

- Maximum available test budget
- Maximum test duration
- Geographic limitations
- Regulatory or platform restrictions
- Whether cold outreach is permitted
- Whether the operator can conduct calls, demos, or fulfillment manually
- Available domains, email accounts, social accounts, marketplace accounts, or community memberships

Default budget assumption:

```text
Maximum unapproved distribution testing budget: $200 total
```

## 4. Competitor and comparable-provider list

For each competitor or comparable provider:

- Name
- URL or profile
- Product category
- Target customer
- Geography
- Relevant channels to investigate
- Date checked

# RESEARCH STEPS

## Step 1: Define the initial distribution hypothesis

Create a short hypothesis:

> “The first qualified customers can likely be reached through [channel] because [evidence or reason], using [message/offer], within [timeframe].”

Each proposed channel must be labeled:

- `VERIFIED`
- `DIRECTIONAL`
- `HYPOTHESIS`
- `UNKNOWN`

Do not present assumptions as findings.

---

## Step 2: Research where competitors appear to acquire customers

Investigate observable acquisition signals across the following channel categories.

### A. Direct outreach

Look for:

- Public sales contact information
- Sales representatives
- Demo or consultation calls
- Prospecting language
- LinkedIn activity from sales or founder accounts
- Public outbound case studies
- Email capture and lead magnets
- Follow-up or booking flows

Evidence examples:

- A competitor actively contacts prospects from identifiable sales accounts
- A public “book a demo” process
- Multiple buyer-facing sales representatives
- A visible outbound campaign or prospecting post

Not sufficient by itself:

- A contact form
- An email address
- A generic “contact us” page

These indicate lead capture, not necessarily customer acquisition.

### B. Organic search

Look for:

- Ranking pages for buyer-intent terms
- Consistent educational content
- Comparison pages
- Programmatic landing pages
- Search traffic estimates from reputable tools
- Backlinks or referring domains
- Search-driven product reviews

Record:

- Search query
- Ranking URL
- Estimated traffic, if available
- Commercial intent
- Evidence date
- Source reliability

### C. Local and map-based channels

Look for:

- Google Business Profiles
- Local directory listings
- Reviews
- Local landing pages
- Community sponsorships
- Chamber or trade association listings
- Local service marketplaces
- Evidence of geographic concentration

Strong signals include:

- Recent, repeated customer reviews
- Active local profiles
- Consistent review velocity
- Calls, bookings, or quote requests visibly supported by the listing

### D. Marketplaces and directories

Investigate relevant:

- Product marketplaces
- Service marketplaces
- App stores
- B2B directories
- Freelancer platforms
- Vertical-specific directories
- Request-for-proposal platforms

Record:

- Number of comparable providers
- Review count and recency
- Listing quality
- Ranking or featured placement
- Customer request volume, if visible
- Fees and approval requirements

### E. Existing communities

Look for customer activity in:

- Reddit
- Facebook Groups
- LinkedIn Groups
- Slack communities
- Discord communities
- Industry forums
- Professional associations
- Local community groups
- Events and meetups

Verify:

- Whether the target buyer is present
- Whether the problem is discussed
- Whether recommendations are allowed
- Whether commercial posts are permitted
- Whether competitors participate
- Whether direct response is possible

Do not treat community membership alone as evidence of acquisition.

### F. Referrals and partnerships

Look for:

- Referral programs
- Affiliate programs
- Integration partners
- Resellers
- Consultants
- Agencies
- Professional associations
- Channel partners
- Public co-marketing activity

Record evidence of:

- Referral incentives
- Partner pages
- Co-branded content
- Shared customer case studies
- Repeat partner relationships

### G. Paid acquisition

Paid channels should be investigated but tested only after lower-cost options unless strong evidence justifies otherwise.

Check for:

- Search ads
- Social ads
- Sponsored listings
- Retargeting
- Sponsorships
- Newsletter ads
- Influencer placements

Evidence sources may include:

- Public ad libraries
- Search-result observation
- Sponsored content disclosures
- Marketplace promotion labels
- Competitor landing pages connected to ads

A visible ad proves that an ad exists, not that it is profitable.

---

## Step 3: Score channel evidence

Score every channel on a 0–3 scale.

| Score | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 | No evidence found |
| 1 | Plausible hypothesis only |
| 2 | Observable activity or directional evidence |
| 3 | Multiple independent, verifiable signals of buyer acquisition |

A channel may be marked `VERIFIED` only when it has at least one strong direct signal or multiple independent signals.

Examples of stronger evidence:

- Competitor-specific landing page linked to a visible ad
- High review velocity on a relevant marketplace
- Repeated buyer requests in a community
- Public case studies naming a channel
- Multiple competitors consistently using the same channel
- A live funnel that can be traced from source to booking, inquiry, or purchase

---

## Step 4: Identify the cheapest and fastest testable channel

Rank channels using:

```text
Channel Priority =
Evidence Strength
× Buyer Access
× Test Speed
× Learning Value
÷ Cost and Operational Friction
```

Use qualitative ratings where exact data is unavailable.

Prefer, in order:

1. Existing customer or professional referrals
2. Direct outreach to a tightly defined buyer list
3. Relevant existing communities
4. Local listings and local outreach
5. Marketplaces and directories
6. Partnerships
7. Organic content or SEO
8. Paid acquisition

This order may be overridden when a channel has unusually strong verified evidence and low test cost.

The selected first channel must be:

- Reachable without substantial spend
- Capable of producing a response within 7–14 days
- Narrow enough to measure
- Compatible with the current offer
- Legally and platform compliant
- Able to produce a meaningful customer signal, not merely impressions

---

## Step 5: Design the first test

The first test must be a **minimum viable distribution experiment**, not a broad launch.

It must specify:

- Target audience
- Exact list or source of prospects
- Channel
- Number of attempts or placements
- Offer
- Message
- Call to action
- Follow-up sequence
- Landing page or booking mechanism
- Test duration
- Cost ceiling
- Success metric
- Stop conditions

### Default first test

Unless stronger evidence supports another channel:

> Conduct a manually researched, personalized outreach test to 25–50 tightly matched prospects or referral sources over 7–10 days.

Suggested structure:

- 25–50 qualified prospects
- 1 personalized initial message
- 1–2 follow-ups
- One clear low-friction CTA
- Manual tracking in a spreadsheet
- No paid automation
- No large content campaign
- No paid ads

Examples of CTAs:

- “Would a 15-minute workflow review be useful?”
- “Are you currently solving this problem another way?”
- “Would you be open to testing a manual version with your team?”
- “Can I send a two-minute example tailored to your situation?”

The CTA should seek a meaningful next step, not vague awareness.

---

## Step 6: Define required assets and messages

Minimum assets:

- One-sentence positioning statement
- Customer-specific pain statement
- One clear offer
- Short outreach message
- Follow-up message
- Proof or credibility element
- Simple landing page, form, calendar, or reply-based workflow
- Objection-handling notes
- Tracking sheet

### Required message structure

1. Relevant observation about the recipient
2. Specific problem or outcome
3. Why the sender is contacting them
4. Concrete offer
5. Low-friction CTA
6. Opt-out or respectful close where appropriate

Do not use unsupported claims such as:

- “Industry-leading”
- “Guaranteed”
- “Everyone needs this”
- “Our competitors use this channel successfully”

---

## Step 7: Run the test and collect evidence

Track each attempt individually:

- Prospect/source
- Qualification status
- Date contacted
- Message version
- Channel
- Delivered or visible
- Opened, if available
- Replied
- Positive reply
- Qualified conversation
- Meeting booked
- Proposal or trial
- Payment or deposit
- Objection
- Follow-up date
- Final outcome

Evidence must include raw artifacts where possible:

- Screenshots
- URLs
- Ad-library records
- Community posts
- Search-result captures
- Marketplace listings
- Outreach log
- Replies
- Meeting notes
- Payment records
- Referral introductions

# TEST RULES

## 1. Budget rules

Default total unapproved budget:

```text
$200 maximum
```

Recommended allocation:

| Use | Maximum |
|---|---:|
| Domain, email, or basic landing-page needs | $50 |
| Marketplace or directory fees | $50 |
| Local or community test costs | $25 |
| Small experiment, sample, or fulfillment cost | $50 |
| Contingency | $25 |

Do not spend the full $200 automatically. The budget is a ceiling, not a target.

Default first-test spend:

```text
$0–$50
```

Paid ads should not be used in the first test unless:

- The channel is strongly verified
- The offer and landing page are already clear
- A qualified audience can be narrowly targeted
- The test has a strict spend cap
- Human approval is obtained where required

## 2. One-variable rule

The first test should change only one major variable:

- Channel
- Audience
- Message
- Offer
- CTA

Do not simultaneously change all five. If the test fails, the system must be able to identify what was learned.

## 3. Minimum sample rules

Use minimum sample sizes appropriate to the channel:

- Direct outreach: 25–50 qualified contacts
- Community post: 1–3 relevant communities, subject to rules
- Referral test: 5–10 credible referral sources
- Marketplace listing: one optimized listing for at least 7–14 days
- Local test: 10–25 qualified local businesses or households, as appropriate
- Paid test: only with a predefined impression, click, or lead threshold

A small sample may generate a useful qualitative signal but cannot justify scaling by itself.

## 4. No vanity-metric rule

The primary metric must represent buyer intent.

Prefer:

- Qualified positive reply rate
- Qualified conversations
- Meetings held
- Trials started
- Deposits
- Paid conversions
- Referral introductions

Do not use impressions, likes, followers, or traffic as the primary success metric unless they directly lead to a validated action.

## 5. Evidence integrity rules

Every claim must be labeled:

- `Observed`
- `Reported by source`
- `Inferred`
- `Hypothesis`

The system must not:

- Invent competitor channels
- Infer profitability from visibility
- Treat a social account as an acquisition channel without buyer-response evidence
- Treat a high ranking as proof of conversions
- Treat a competitor’s paid ad as proof of positive ROI
- Use unverifiable traffic or revenue estimates as facts

## 6. Stop-loss rule

Stop a test immediately if:

- It violates platform or legal rules
- The audience is clearly unqualified
- The offer creates misleading expectations
- Spend reaches the test cap without sufficient learning
- Negative feedback indicates a positioning or trust problem
- Manual fulfillment cannot support resulting demand

---

# KILL / CONTINUE / SCALE RULES

Thresholds should be adjusted for price, sales cycle, and channel, but the following defaults apply.

## KILL

Kill the channel or current test version when any of the following is true:

- 25–50 qualified outreach attempts produce **zero relevant replies**
- The channel produces no qualified conversation after the minimum sample
- The audience repeatedly indicates the problem is not a priority
- The cost per qualified conversation is clearly uneconomic relative to expected customer value
- The channel requires substantial spend before producing a measurable signal
- The message receives attention but no buyer-intent action after one reasonable revision
- The channel cannot be measured reliably

Killing a test does not necessarily kill the opportunity. Record the reason and preserve any useful objections or language.

## CONTINUE

Continue with a revised test when:

- At least one qualified buyer expresses meaningful interest
- There are repeated relevant replies but insufficient volume
- The offer is understood but the CTA or timing is weak
- Buyers identify a different urgent use case
- The test produces strong qualitative evidence but too few observations
- There are qualified conversations but no close due to a solvable trust, pricing, or fulfillment issue

A continuation test may change one of:

- Message
- CTA
- Segment
- Offer
- Follow-up timing
- Proof asset

Recommended continuation budget:

```text
Additional $25–$75, remaining within the $200 limit unless approved.
```

## SCALE

Scale only when the test produces both quantitative and qualitative evidence.

Minimum default scale evidence for direct outreach:

- At least 50 qualified contacts, or equivalent buyer exposure
- At least 3 qualified conversations
- At least 2 serious next steps, such as trial, proposal, or demo
- At least 1 payment, deposit, signed commitment, or repeated strong buying signal
- A documented reason buyers responded
- A repeatable message and audience definition
- Fulfillment capacity confirmed
- Acquisition cost estimate within acceptable economics

For lower-volume or high-ticket offers, substitute equivalent evidence such as:

- Multiple independent buyer commitments
- Signed pilots
- Paid discovery
- Purchase orders
- Strong referral commitments

Scaling means increasing controlled volume, not immediately launching broad paid acquisition.

Initial scaling actions should be:

1. Repeat the winning message with a larger but similar audience.
2. Add one adjacent segment.
3. Improve tracking and follow-up.
4. Document the sales process.
5. Test a second low-cost channel.
6. Seek human approval before material spend increases.

# HUMAN APPROVAL GATES

## Gate 1: Before paid acquisition

Human approval is required before:

- Any paid advertising campaign
- Sponsorship purchases
- Influencer payments
- Paid lead lists
- Automated outreach tools with recurring fees
- Spend above $50 on a single channel

Required approval packet:

- Channel evidence
- Test objective
- Audience definition
- Creative or message
- Landing page
- Budget cap
- Primary metric
- Kill threshold
- Expected learning

## Gate 2: Before exceeding $200 total

No additional distribution spend may be committed once cumulative spend reaches $200 without explicit human approval.

Approval must include:

- Total spend to date
- Results by channel
- Qualified buyer evidence
- Cost per qualified action
- Remaining uncertainty
- Proposed next test
- Why additional spend is justified

## Gate 3: Before scaling volume

Human approval is required before:

- Sending more than 100 outbound messages in a test cycle
- Hiring sales or marketing support
- Purchasing substantial software
- Expanding to multiple paid channels
- Increasing spend by more than 2×
- Entering a new geography
- Making public claims based on early results

## Gate 4: Before operational commitments

Approval is required before:

- Long-term contracts
- Annual software subscriptions
- Marketplace packages
- Inventory or production commitments
- Paid partnerships
- Agency retainers
- Commitments that cannot be recovered if the channel fails

## Gate 5: Before declaring distribution validated

Distribution may be marked `VALIDATED` only when:

- The acquisition channel is documented
- The target audience is specific
- The message and offer are repeatable
- Buyer-intent metrics have been recorded
- At least one meaningful commercial outcome exists
- Evidence is stored and auditable
- Costs and operational requirements are understood
- A human approves the transition from testing to scaling

The stage should end with one of four statuses:

```text
KILL
CONTINUE
SCALE
BLOCKED — HUMAN APPROVAL REQUIRED
```

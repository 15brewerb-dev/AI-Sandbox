# Market Evidence Rubric v1.0

## 0. Decision standard

An opportunity passes only when:

1. Every mandatory gate passes.
2. No disqualifier applies.
3. The opportunity scores at least **70/100**.
4. It scores at least the minimum required points in every critical category.
5. Evidence is recent, attributable, and independently verifiable.

Exciting descriptions, large market-size estimates, trend articles, search volume, social engagement, or founder intuition are not sufficient evidence by themselves.

---

# 1. Evidence standards

## 1.1 Evidence quality tiers

| Tier | Evidence | Reliability |
|---|---|---:|
| A | Actual purchase, paid invoice, active customer case, public pricing with identifiable buyer, transaction marketplace data, or direct buyer commitment | Strong |
| B | Competitor with active customers, recurring reviews mentioning the problem, job postings, procurement requests, buyer interviews with specific current spending | Moderate |
| C | Search volume, social comments, waitlists, likes, trend reports, surveys asking hypothetical questions | Weak |
| D | Founder opinion, generic TAM claims, unsupported forecasts, “people would pay,” or AI-generated market claims | Unacceptable |

At least one **Tier A** demand or pricing signal is required to pass.

## 1.2 Evidence requirements

Every evidence item must include:

- Source URL or file reference.
- Date collected.
- Date of the underlying activity, if available.
- What specifically it proves.
- Whether it is first-party, third-party, or self-reported.
- Any relevant limitations.
- Researcher confidence: high, medium, or low.

Evidence older than **24 months** should not be used as primary evidence unless the market is demonstrably stable.

## 1.3 Independence rule

Repeated claims from the same company, affiliate network, or copied article count as **one evidence source**, not multiple sources.

---

# 2. Mandatory PASS/REJECT gates

All gates below must pass.

## Gate 1: Specific paying customer

**Pass if:**

- The buyer is a specific identifiable segment, not “everyone” or “businesses.”
- The buyer has a defined recurring or urgent job.
- The buyer can be reached through at least one observable channel.
- At least **3 independent examples** show that this customer type currently pays for a related solution.

**Reject if:**

- The proposed customer is undefined.
- The problem is described only as a general inconvenience.
- Evidence consists only of hypothetical survey responses or trend commentary.

---

## Gate 2: Proven demand

Pass if one of the following is documented:

### Strong path

- At least **3 paying competitors or substitutes** with evidence of active customers; or
- At least **10 recent buyer transactions** for comparable products or services; or
- At least **3 direct buyer commitments**, deposits, purchase orders, or signed pilots.

### Acceptable early-stage path

- At least **2 paying competitors or substitutes**, plus:
  - **10 buyer interviews or conversations**, and
  - At least **3 buyers** reporting that they currently spend money, staff time, or external fees on the problem.

The evidence must show current behavior, not merely interest.

**Reject if:**

- No one can be shown to pay for the problem or a close substitute.
- Demand depends primarily on a predicted future trend.
- The only signal is views, likes, search interest, newsletter subscriptions, or free usage.

---

## Gate 3: Buyer willingness to pay

Pass if at least one of the following exists:

- Publicly observable comparable pricing from **3 or more providers**.
- At least **3 buyers** disclose current spend for the same or closely related outcome.
- At least **3 qualified buyers** agree to a paid pilot, deposit, purchase order, or specific next step at a stated price.
- A transaction marketplace shows at least **10 completed purchases** at a comparable price range.

A buyer saying “I would consider it” does not qualify.

The proposed initial price must be supported by at least one comparable transaction or explicit buyer commitment.

**Reject if:**

- Pricing is based only on cost-plus markup, competitor aspiration, or TAM math.
- Buyers require extensive education before understanding the value.
- The buyer is not the economic decision-maker and no purchasing path is identified.

---

## Gate 4: Economically viable unit

The opportunity must have a plausible initial unit model.

Pass if, using conservative assumptions:

- Gross margin is projected at **50% or higher** after direct fulfillment costs; and
- Customer acquisition cost can plausibly be recovered within **6 months** or **3 purchases**; and
- The first customer can be served without requiring a large fixed investment; and
- The proposed price is at least **3 times variable delivery cost**, including contractor, API, software, payment, and support costs.

For service businesses, founder labor must be included at a realistic hourly rate.

If exact costs are unknown, the Researcher must use a sensitivity range and pass only if the base case and downside case remain viable.

**Reject if:**

- The model requires scale before becoming economically viable.
- Fulfillment cost is unknown or likely to exceed the selling price.
- The opportunity depends on unpaid labor or permanently subsidized acquisition.

---

## Gate 5: Fulfillment feasibility

Pass if:

- A minimum viable deliverable can be produced within **14 calendar days**.
- The first version can be delivered with existing tools, contractors, or manual processes.
- AI or automation can reduce delivery time, labor, or error rate by at least **30%**, or materially increase output per operator.
- Human review can be added for consequential outputs.
- Required data, integrations, permissions, and inputs are available.

**Reject if:**

- The service requires unproven technical research before the first sale.
- The value depends on fully autonomous AI accuracy where errors create material harm.
- Required data is inaccessible, illegal to obtain, or dependent on unreliable third-party access.
- Every customer requires a substantially different custom build.

---

## Gate 6: Observable distribution

Pass if at least one acquisition channel meets all three conditions:

1. The channel is observable: specific communities, directories, search terms, marketplaces, outbound lists, partners, events, or platforms can be named.
2. The target customer is reachable through that channel.
3. A test can be run for **14 days or less** with a budget of **$500 or less**, excluding founder labor.

The Researcher must specify:

- Channel.
- Customer list or audience source.
- Message or offer.
- Expected number of contacts or impressions.
- Success metric.
- Test cost.
- Follow-up process.

**Reject if:**

- Distribution depends on “going viral,” broad brand awareness, or an unnamed audience.
- Customer acquisition requires a large advertising budget before learning is possible.
- The only stated strategy is “SEO,” “social media,” or “partnerships” without a concrete test.

---

## Gate 7: Legal, ethical, and IP safety

Pass if:

- The business does not copy protected branding, artwork, code, content, or proprietary data.
- Data collection and processing have a lawful basis.
- Claims can be substantiated.
- Required licenses, insurance, certifications, and professional oversight are identified.
- No material regulatory barrier prevents a small validation test.

**Reject if:**

- The model depends on impersonation, counterfeit goods, scraping restricted data, copyright infringement, or trademark confusion.
- The product requires regulated advice or decisions without qualified oversight.
- The opportunity relies on market manipulation, insider information, or speculative trading.
- The business cannot legally deliver the promised outcome in the target market.

---

## Gate 8: Fast validation path

Pass if a paid or behavior-based test can be launched within **14 days**.

The test must seek one of:

- Payment.
- Deposit.
- Signed pilot.
- Purchase order.
- Scheduled qualified sales call with a documented budget and buying timeline.
- Completed customer workflow using the proposed offer.

A landing-page email signup alone does not qualify as validation.

**Reject if:**

- Validation requires months of product development.
- The buyer’s purchase cycle is unknown and likely longer than six months.
- No measurable test can distinguish demand from curiosity.

# 3. Required evidence by category

## 3.1 Demand evidence

The Researcher must provide:

- At least **3 comparable paid solutions** or **10 comparable transactions**.
- Customer segment and use case for each.
- Price or transaction value where available.
- Evidence that the problem is current.
- Evidence of frequency, urgency, or financial consequence.
- At least one substitute customers use today, including “do nothing” if relevant.

Preferred evidence:

- Active paid listings.
- Current vendor pricing pages.
- Reviews dated within the last 24 months.
- Procurement postings.
- Job postings showing repeated internal labor for the task.
- Buyer interviews describing current spending and dissatisfaction.

Insufficient evidence:

- “The market is growing.”
- “People complain online.”
- “There are millions of potential customers.”
- High search volume without purchase evidence.
- Free tools with no evidence of conversion or commercial demand.

---

## 3.2 Pricing and willingness-to-pay evidence

The Researcher must provide:

- A proposed initial price.
- A price range supported by at least **3 comparable paid offers**.
- Expected purchase frequency.
- Buyer or budget owner.
- Reason the proposed offer can command that price.
- Any setup, support, fulfillment, or refund costs.

Preferred evidence:

- Recent customer invoices.
- Public checkout pages.
- Marketplace completed-sale data.
- Paid pilot commitments.
- Buyer statements with current spend and budget authority.

Required pricing test:

- Present a specific offer and price to at least **10 qualified prospects**, or
- Obtain at least **3 paid commitments or deposits**.

---

## 3.3 Fulfillment evidence

The Researcher must provide:

- Step-by-step delivery workflow.
- Required inputs.
- Tools, APIs, contractors, and human review points.
- Estimated labor time per customer.
- Direct cost per customer.
- Expected quality standard.
- Failure and escalation process.
- Data security and retention requirements.
- What AI or automation performs and what remains human-controlled.

The workflow must be tested on at least **one representative sample** before final approval where technically possible.

---

## 3.4 Distribution evidence

The Researcher must provide:

- One primary channel and one backup channel.
- Evidence that target buyers are active in each channel.
- A reachable list, audience, marketplace, keyword set, or partner type.
- Outreach or acquisition script.
- Test budget and duration.
- Target metrics.

Minimum initial test metrics should include:

- Number of targeted contacts or impressions.
- Response rate.
- Qualified conversation rate.
- Offer acceptance rate.
- Paid conversion rate.
- Cost per qualified lead.
- Cost per customer, if available.

A channel is not validated by reach alone. It must produce qualified buyer behavior.

---

# 4. Disqualifiers

Reject immediately if any of the following applies:

1. No identifiable buyer or economic decision-maker.
2. No evidence of current payment for the problem or a close substitute.
3. Reliance on speculative trading, gambling, arbitrage, or beating professional institutions.
4. Copying protected IP, brand identity, proprietary data, or copyrighted assets.
5. Illegal, deceptive, fraudulent, or materially unethical customer acquisition.
6. Required regulation, licensing, or professional liability is ignored.
7. Customer harm from predictable AI errors cannot be controlled.
8. The model depends on inaccessible, unlawfully obtained, or unstable data.
9. The business requires more than **$25,000** before the first meaningful demand test without exceptional evidence.
10. Gross margin is likely below **30%** after direct costs.
11. Acquisition depends on virality, a single platform, or an unconfirmed partnership.
12. The opportunity cannot be tested with a concrete offer within **14 days**.
13. The main value proposition is a vague improvement with no measurable customer outcome.
14. Evidence is fabricated, unverifiable, duplicated, or materially misrepresented.
15. The opportunity requires customers to change behavior without a clear financial, operational, or emotional payoff.

# 5. 100-point scorecard

A score does not override a failed mandatory gate or disqualifier.

## A. Proven demand — 25 points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| 3+ paying competitors or 10+ relevant transactions | 8 |
| Evidence of repeated or recurring need | 5 |
| Clear pain, urgency, cost, or risk of inaction | 5 |
| Evidence from at least 3 independent source types | 4 |
| Recent evidence, with most activity within 24 months | 3 |

**Minimum to pass: 15/25**

---

## B. Pricing and willingness to pay — 15 points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Comparable prices documented from 3+ providers or transactions | 5 |
| Proposed price tied to a specific buyer budget or current spend | 4 |
| At least 3 qualified buyers show purchase intent at a stated price | 3 |
| Repeat purchase, subscription, or expansion potential | 3 |

**Minimum to pass: 9/15**

---

## C. Fulfillment and AI leverage — 20 points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| MVP deliverable can be produced within 14 days | 4 |
| AI/automation reduces labor, time, or errors by at least 30% | 5 |
| Workflow and required inputs are documented | 4 |
| Human QA and failure handling are practical | 3 |
| Delivery cost and quality are measurable | 4 |

**Minimum to pass: 12/20**

---

## D. Distribution and acquisition — 20 points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Specific reachable channel with evidence of target buyers | 5 |
| 14-day acquisition test is defined | 5 |
| Test can run for $500 or less | 3 |
| Outreach, offer, and follow-up process are specified | 3 |
| Acquisition economics have a plausible path to profitability | 4 |

**Minimum to pass: 12/20**

---

## E. Unit economics and startup cost — 10 points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| Gross margin is projected at 50%+ | 3 |
| First meaningful test costs less than $2,500 | 2 |
| CAC recovery is plausible within 6 months or 3 purchases | 3 |
| No large fixed investment is required before validation | 2 |

**Minimum to pass: 6/10**

---

## F. Risk, legality, and repeatability — 10 points

| Criterion | Points |
|---|---:|
| No material IP, legal, or regulatory exposure | 3 |
| Data, platform, and supplier dependencies are controlled | 2 |
| Offer can be standardized across customers | 3 |
| Clear path to repeatable revenue rather than one-off work | 2 |

**Minimum to pass: 6/10**

---

# 6. Decision classes

## PASS

An opportunity is **PASS** only if:

- Total score is **70 or higher**.
- All category minimums are met.
- All mandatory gates pass.
- No disqualifier applies.
- The first validation test is specified and can begin within 14 days.

## REJECT

An opportunity is **REJECT** if:

- Any mandatory gate fails.
- Any disqualifier applies.
- Total score is below 70.
- Any category falls below its minimum.
- Evidence is insufficient, stale, unverifiable, or materially contradictory.

## HOLD — evidence incomplete

“HOLD” is permitted only for missing research, not for weak evidence. The Researcher must specify:

- Exact missing evidence.
- Source or experiment needed.
- Owner.
- Deadline, no longer than 7 days.
- Pass/fail threshold.

If the evidence is not obtained by the deadline, the opportunity becomes **REJECT**.

# 8. Final machine-readable decision rule

```python
def decide(opportunity):
    mandatory_gates = [
        opportunity.gates.specific_paying_customer,
        opportunity.gates.proven_demand,
        opportunity.gates.willingness_to_pay,
        opportunity.gates.unit_economics,
        opportunity.gates.fulfillment,
        opportunity.gates.distribution,
        opportunity.gates.legal_ip_safety,
        opportunity.gates.fast_validation_path,
    ]

    minimum_scores = {
        "demand": 15,
        "pricing": 9,
        "fulfillment": 12,
        "distribution": 12,
        "economics": 6,
        "risk_repeatability": 6,
    }

    critical_evidence_present = (
        at_least_one_tier_A_demand_or_pricing_signal(opportunity)
        and evidence_is_verifiable(opportunity)
        and evidence_is_not_materially_stale(opportunity)
    )

    if opportunity.disqualifiers.present:
        return "REJECT"

    if any(gate != "pass" for gate in mandatory_gates):
        return "REJECT"

    if not critical_evidence_present:
        return "REJECT"

    if opportunity.score.total < 70:
        return "REJECT"

    for category, minimum in minimum_scores.items():
        if opportunity.score[category] < minimum:
            return "REJECT"

    if not validation_test_can_start_within_14_days(opportunity):
        return "REJECT"

    return "PASS"
```

The Manager should not advance a **PASS** opportunity to execution without the documented validation test, owner, budget, offer, and success threshold.
